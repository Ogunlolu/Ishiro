# Ishiro — brand direction (draft v1)

Status: colour direction approved 18 Nov 2026 (Guava pink, black and white, trip photo); the rest is still a proposal. Visual board and sample
screens: https://claude.ai/artifact/BS92wjiJh4MF1Gyx1mzChF (private to the owner).

## Who it is for

Friends aged 18–38 on group trips. Launch markets: North America and the UK.

## Promise

**Split the trip. Keep the friends.**

Everyone sees the same numbers, the person who paid gets thanked, and the trip
ends square. Trust is the foundation; fun is the reason people choose it.

## What we take from apps that worked

These are my readings of well-known products, not cited research.

| From | What worked | What Ishiro takes |
|---|---|---|
| Strava | Kudos, a feed of activities, shareable cards; a plain white app with one accent colour | Reactions on each expense; the trip as a feed; one unmistakable brand colour |
| Spotify Wrapped | Year-end recap as bold, screenshot-ready cards with playful labels | Trip Wrapped with awards, built to share in the group chat |
| Duolingo | A distinct voice and small celebrations | Celebrate settling up. We do not copy the guilt-trip reminders |
| Monzo (UK) | Plain human language about money; a colour nobody else had | Plain words, never bank language; an ownable colour |
| Cash App, Venmo (North America) | Amount-first entry; payments as social moments | Big-number keypad first; expenses read as "Ada got dinner" |
| Partiful | Link-first, no-download-needed group organising that feels like a party | Join a trip from a link, no account |
| Splitwise, Tricount, Splid | Fewest-payments settling, join by link, offline | All of it, as the functional base |

What we deliberately avoid: red/green debt colouring, daily limits and ads
(Splitwise's weak spot), shaming whoever owes, and leaderboards of debt.

## Personality and voice

The fun friend who is good with money: warm, witty but never petty, fair to
everyone. Celebrates the payer; never shames the person who owes.

- "Ada got dinner." not "New expense added by Ada."
- "Ben, you're up. You've fronted the least." not "Ben has an outstanding debt."
- "All square. Trip closed clean." not "All balances have been settled."

Rules: short sentences; the person's name first; "gets back" and "owes", never
"debt" or "debtor"; jokes point at the trip, never at someone's balance. Spelling
and currency follow the user's locale (US, Canada, UK). Each trip has a tone
setting, Playful (default) or Plain, for groups where jokes about money would
land badly.

## What matters, in order

1. Add an expense in 10 seconds. If it is slow, nobody logs anything.
2. One number: where do I stand? Visible the moment the trip opens.
3. Every number explains itself. Tap it, see what it is made of.
4. Then, fun: in the voice and the moments, never in decoration.

## Visual identity

**95% calm, 5% party.** (Revised after review: the first version used too many
colours and was hard to focus on.) Day to day the app is black, white and
quiet so the numbers are all you see. Fun lives in the words and in a few
earned moments: a thank-you, the stamp, the wrap-up.

- **Wordmark:** "ishiro", lowercase, in Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold.
- **Colour:** three only.

  | Name | Hex | Use |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ink | `#111111` | Text and numbers |
  | Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Everything else |
  | Guava | `#FF4F8B` | The one main action per screen, and celebrations (always with Ink text) |

  Supporting greys: `#555555` for secondary text, `#E5E5E5` for dividers.
  No colour-coded people and no red or green balances; who owes is said in
  words ("you get back", "Ben pays you").
- **Type:** Bricolage Grotesque for the big numbers, headlines and the stamp;
  each phone's system font for everything else.
- **Layout:** one job and one main button per screen; lists divided by thin
  lines rather than boxed cards; generous white space.
- **The stamp:** a passport-style "Closed clean" mark, earned when a trip is
  settled. Used sparingly so it stays special.
- **Trip photo:** every trip has a cover photo; it opens the trip home screen, appears as a thumbnail on other screens, and is the only colour besides the accent.
- **The party moment:** Trip Wrapped is the trip photo full screen with the
  total in Guava, because it is the screen people share.
- **Motion and touch:** quick (150–300 ms); a stamp "thunk" with a light
  vibration when a payment is marked done. Honours reduced-motion settings.
- **Accessibility:** text contrast at least 4.5:1, touch targets at least
  44 pt, works with large system text, no meaning carried by colour alone.

## Signature moments

1. **Reactions and banter** — friends react to an expense with emoji and
   leave a one-line comment ("who ordered the third bottle"). This replaced
   the earlier "say thanks" idea, which felt forced.
2. **Whose turn** — "Ben, you're up": a gentle prompt that keeps things even.
3. **Square up** — few payments, a "Why $90?" explanation on each, a progress
   bar, and the Closed clean stamp when everyone is even.
4. **Trip Wrapped** — shareable recap with awards (The Banker, Uber Royalty,
   Minister of Snacks, Fastest to square up).

## Trips with several cities

- A trip can have several stops, each with its own photo.
- Trip home shows the stops as swipeable photo cards, with the current city in
  the middle and the others peeking in at the sides.
- Under the cards, a three-part bar: finished cities in black, the current
  one in Guava, upcoming ones in grey, each with what was spent there.
- There is still one balance for the whole trip; people settle once, at the end.
- Expenses are filed under the city the group is in, and can be moved.
- An expense shows the amount as paid (for example €96) with the trip
  currency underneath ($104).
- Trip Wrapped adds spend per city.
